Smart MIFC

Exploring a more digital and adaptable view of material and information flow.

Project summary

Exploring how Material and Information Flow Charts (MIFC) might evolve to represent digital information, decision points, and timing in modern manufacturing systems.

This is an evolving concept summary. Add validated outcomes and live links as the work develops.

Problem Statement

Traditional Material and Information Flow Charts provide a useful view of production systems. As operations become more connected, a static picture may not make the timing of information, decision ownership, or digital signals sufficiently visible.

Approach

Start with TPS and conventional MIFC principles. Explore how the model could show material flow, information flow, decision points, and the moments when information becomes available or actionable.

Scope

A conceptual model for selected manufacturing scenarios—not a deployed production system. The focus is on representation, communication, and practical interpretation.

Actions

  1. Review traditional MIFC methods and example scenarios.
  2. Identify where information timing and decision points are difficult to see.
  3. Sketch a digital-aware flow representation.
  4. Test the concept against simplified manufacturing examples and gather feedback.

Impact

Potential value: clearer conversations about process flow, information availability, and decisions. No operational performance improvement is claimed or measured at this stage.

Lessons Learned

Working reflection: better visualization is useful only if it makes work easier to understand and decisions easier to discuss. Add specific lessons here as the concept is tested.

Next Steps

  • Refine the model and visual language.
  • Develop a focused example or interactive prototype.
  • Collect feedback from practitioners.
  • Document limitations, observations, and revisions.
